AWS Developer Certification Details

The AWS Certified Developer – Associate validates skills for developing, testing, deploying, and debugging cloud applications that use AWS services. It is the closer match when you want to demonstrate application development skills rather than lead with architecture design.

Exam structure and objectives

The guide also lists architecture design, designing CI/CD pipelines, and designing AWS networking infrastructure among tasks outside the target candidate’s scope. The exam includes architectural patterns and AWS service knowledge where they support application development, but it is not a substitute for the Solutions Architect exam’s design focus. See the full DVA-C02 exam guide for the complete objective and service lists.

Recommended background

AWS describes the target candidate as having at least one year of hands-on experience developing and maintaining applications with AWS services, and recommends proficiency in at least one programming language. That experience describes the expected candidate profile; AWS says certifications have no specific prerequisites. Training courses are recommended for preparation but are not mandatory. Read AWS’s eligibility policy and the exam guide rather than treating the experience recommendation as a hard entry requirement.

AWS offers Solutions Architect certifications at Associate and Professional levels. Both focus on evaluating requirements and designing solutions, but the Professional exam targets more complex systems and a broader organizational context.

Professional: SAP-C02

The Professional credential is for architects working with more advanced designs. AWS describes the target candidate as having at least two years of experience using AWS services to design and implement cloud solutions and guiding architectural decisions across multiple applications or projects.

As of October 4, 2026, the SAP-C02 guide groups its objectives into four different domains from SAA-C03:

  • Design Solutions for Organizational Complexity (26%)
  • Design for New Solutions (29%)
  • Continuous Improvement for Existing Solutions (25%)
  • Accelerate Workload Migration and Modernization (20%)

These domains explain why the Professional exam is not simply a longer version of the Associate exam. It tests how to shape and improve solutions across complex environments. The current SAP-C02 exam guide provides its full scope. AWS’s testing eligibility policy says certifications have no specific prerequisites, so holding SAA is not a formal requirement before attempting the Professional exam. AWS’s recommended experience for SAP-C02 is substantially greater, however, and candidates should use that profile to assess readiness.

AWS Developer vs Solutions Architect: What Changes?

The Associate exams share AWS services and both assess security. Their central question differs:

  • Developer – Associate: Can you build, package, deploy, secure, and troubleshoot an application that uses AWS services?
  • Solutions Architect – Associate: Can you select and combine AWS services into an architecture that meets stated constraints?

For example, in a serverless application, the Developer exam aligns more closely with implementing and debugging function code, calling AWS APIs, handling errors, and deploying changes. The Solutions Architect exam aligns more closely with choosing how the application is exposed, how it stores data, how access is controlled, and how the design balances availability, performance, and cost. Real jobs often mix these responsibilities; the exam choice is about which skills you want the credential to validate.

Choosing the Right Certification

Start with the work you want to do

Look at the responsibilities you handle now or want in your next role. Choose Developer – Associate if application code, AWS APIs and SDKs, tests, CI/CD workflows, and application troubleshooting are frequent tasks. Choose Solutions Architect – Associate if your work involves requirements analysis, service selection, workload design, security and resilience trade-offs, or explaining architectural choices.

Consider Solutions Architect – Professional when you already have substantial experience designing and improving AWS solutions and need to demonstrate work with complex, cross-application or migration scenarios. A Professional-level title alone is not a reason to skip the experience and skills described in its exam guide.

Use the exam objectives as a checklist

Read the current exam guide before buying a course or booking a test. Compare its objectives with skills you can demonstrate in a real project. A Developer candidate could build and operate a small application that uses an AWS SDK, applies least-privilege access, records useful logs, handles errors, and has a repeatable deployment process. A Solutions Architect candidate could take a workload requirement, propose an architecture, and explain how the design addresses security, resilience, performance, and cost.

These exercises do not replace AWS’s preparation resources, but they reveal whether you can apply the topics rather than recognize their names. AWS recommends hands-on experience for preparation, and its exam pages link to current practice questions and learning plans.

Job Market and Employer Expectations

Treat the certification as one signal

A certification confirms that you passed an exam with a defined scope. It does not by itself establish production experience, determine a job title, or guarantee a job, promotion, or salary. Check job postings in your location and target role: note which certification, if any, they request, and compare those requirements with the exam guide and your practical experience.

Compare compensation for the role, not just the credential

Salary ranges vary with location, seniority, industry, and responsibilities, so a single number for “AWS Developer” or “AWS Solutions Architect” does not make a reliable comparison between these exams. Final Thoughts and Recommendations

The practical difference

Developer – Associate is the more direct fit for people who build and support AWS-backed applications. Solutions Architect – Associate is the more direct fit for people who decide how AWS components should work together to meet requirements. Solutions Architect – Professional covers advanced architecture work across complex solutions and migrations.

A sensible preparation path

  1. Choose the exam that matches the work you want to demonstrate.
  2. Read the latest official exam guide and its domain weights.
  3. Use hands-on work to practice the objectives that are new to you.
  4. Use AWS practice questions to check your understanding, then return to the guide for any gaps.
  5. Confirm the exam version, fees, and delivery options on AWS’s certification page before scheduling.

There is no universal “better” certification. Match the exam to your goals and current skills, then support it with examples of work you can explain clearly.

FAQs

How much do AWS Developers and Solutions Architects make?

There is no reliable universal salary difference tied to these certifications. Pay depends on the role, location, seniority, industry, and employer. Compare compensation for similar jobs in your market, and do not treat an exam credential as a salary guarantee.

Which is better, AWS Developer or Solutions Architect?

Neither is universally better. Developer – Associate emphasizes application development and troubleshooting; Solutions Architect – Associate emphasizes architecture decisions and trade-offs. Pick the exam whose objectives match the work you do or want to do.

Does Solutions Architect – Professional require Solutions Architect – Associate?

No. AWS says its certifications have no specific prerequisites. The Professional exam does have a more advanced target profile, including at least two years of experience designing and implementing AWS cloud solutions, so review the current guide before deciding whether you are ready.

Do I need to take an AWS training course before the exam?

No. AWS recommends training and hands-on experience, but says training is not mandatory for certification. Use the current exam guide to plan your preparation and confirm the latest version before registering.
