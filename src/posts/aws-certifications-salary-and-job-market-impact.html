Short answer: an AWS certification can help when employers in your target role ask for that credential, but earning one does not guarantee a raise, promotion, or job. There is no single salary figure that applies to every AWS-certified worker. Pay depends on the job, location, experience, and industry, so compare compensation for the role you want instead of treating a credential as a promised pay increase.

A certification shows that you passed an exam with a defined scope. Pair it with evidence that you can apply those skills: production work, a well-documented project, or clear examples of decisions you made and problems you solved.

Do AWS certifications increase salary?

A certification does not set your salary. It may help you meet a stated hiring requirement or show that you have studied skills relevant to a role. Salary comparisons between certified and non-certified people do not, on their own, show that the exam caused any difference: the groups may also differ in experience, job level, location, and employer.

To estimate pay for your next role, compare like with like:

  1. Name the job, level, and location. “Cloud engineer” in one market is not directly comparable to “solutions architect” in another.
  2. Review current job postings. Note the salary range, required years of experience, AWS skills, and whether each certification is required, preferred, or not mentioned.
  3. Separate base pay from total compensation. Check whether a range includes bonus, equity, or benefits before comparing offers.
  4. Use local wage data as a baseline. In the United States, the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes wage distributions by occupation, industry, and area. That gives context for a role; it is not an AWS-certification pay premium. Use your country’s equivalent data source elsewhere.
  5. Ask about your employer’s rules. A manager or recruiter can tell you whether a credential changes interview eligibility, promotion requirements, or reimbursement at that company.

The BLS guide to wage data and salary negotiations explains how occupation, industry, experience, and location affect comparisons.

Where a certification can help in the job market

Start with the jobs you want, then look for a repeated credential requirement. A certificate is most useful when it maps to those postings or to a formal requirement at your current employer. It can also give you a structured learning target while you fill a skills gap.

Read the matching AWS exam guide before you study. AWS says its exams sample knowledge and skills from role-related job domains; an exam does not cover every task or setting in that role. Treat a pass as evidence about the exam objectives, then show applied experience separately with work examples, a portfolio, or a technical discussion.

Make the credential useful at work

Choose a credential only after you can explain what work you want it to support. Before studying, make a short plan:

  • Pick one target role and collect several current postings from your market.
  • Mark the recurring AWS services, experience, and credentials those employers request.
  • Compare that list with the exam guide and identify what you still need to practice.
  • Ask whether your employer will cover the exam, provide study time, or use the credential in a documented role or promotion path.
  • Plan a work example that shows how you applied the knowledge, including constraints, trade-offs, and results you can substantiate.

AWS recommends hands-on experience for exam preparation, with guidance ranging from six months to two years depending on the certification level. AWS does not require a training course to sit an exam, and the experience guidance is not a fixed study-hour estimate. Use the target-candidate section in the exam guide to judge your own readiness.

Calculate the cost before you book

AWS’s standard U.S. exam prices are $100 for Business or Foundational exams, $150 for Associate exams, and $300 for Professional or Specialty exams. These prices cover one attempt; taxes may apply, and local-currency prices can differ. Beta exams may have different prices. Check the current AWS exam pricing and policies for the exam and country you plan to use.

Most AWS Certifications are valid for three years. Recertification routes differ by credential and may include passing a current or higher-level exam; Cloud Practitioner also has an AWS Cloud Quest route. AWS has introduced an open-beta training-and-labs option that can maintain selected certifications for one year from the date you complete it. The new validity period is calculated from that completion date, not added on top of the old expiration date. It requires an active AWS Skill Builder subscription and a supported credential within 90 days of expiration. Check the current recertification options for your certification before planning around that route.

Once certified, you can access an AWS 50% discount voucher for recertification or another AWS exam. Add the net exam cost, any training or lab subscription, and your study time to your decision. If the target employers do not request the credential and your budget is limited, building and documenting role-relevant experience may be the stronger next step.

How employers should interpret a certification

AWS exams assess specific, published objectives. They can help an employer understand what topics a candidate has studied, but they do not replace questions about hands-on ability, communication, judgment, or experience with the employer’s systems. Candidates can make the signal more useful by naming the exact certification and level, keeping its status current, and describing related work without claiming the credential alone proves production experience.

Frequently asked questions

Will an AWS certification get me a raise?

It may support a raise discussion if your employer connects that credential to a role, skill requirement, or promotion path. It does not guarantee an increase. Use your employer’s compensation criteria and pay data for the role and location you are discussing.

What is the best AWS certification for a job?

Choose the exam whose objectives match the job you want and your current experience. A developer role, architecture role, and operations role have different exam paths. Compare job postings with the official exam guides before paying for an exam.

How long does an AWS certification last?

AWS certifications generally remain valid for three years. Recertification and the newer training-based maintenance option depend on the credential; see AWS’s current renewal table for the exact route.
